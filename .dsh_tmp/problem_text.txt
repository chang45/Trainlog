Problem
A
：
Multi-Field
Modeling
and
Parameter
Optimization
for
Multi-Fluid
Injection
Development
in
Deep
Unconventional
Reservoirs
China
’
s
recoverable
tight-oil
reserves
are
approximately
17.82
billion
tonnes,
accounting
for
about
40%
of
its
recoverable
petroleum
resources,
and
are
mainly
distributed
in
the
Ordos,
Junggar,
Sichuan,
and
Songliao
basins;
they
constitute
an
important
replacement
resource
for
safeguarding
national
energy
security.
In
deep
tight-oil
reservoirs,
the
matrix
permeability
is
generally
no
higher
than
3
2
0
.
1
1
0
m
m
-

;
the
pore
throats
are
small,
natural
fractures
are
poorly
developed,
and
the
natural
productivity
of
a
single
well
is
low,
so
that
the
recovery
factor
under
conventional
development
is
commonly
below
10%.
In
field
practice,
a
composite
fluid
of
liquid/supercritical
CO
2
plus
a
nano-imbibition
agent
is
used
for
reservoir
stimulation
and
huff-and-puff
development.
The
process
comprises
three
stages:
(1)
high-pressure
injection,
in
which
CO
2
and
the
nano-imbibition
agent
are
injected
into
the
formation
in
a
specified
mixing
ratio
and
hydraulic
fractures
are
created
under
conditions
above
the
formation
breakdown
pressure;
(2)
shut-in
soaking,
during
which
CO
2
diffuses
into
the
matrix
and
dissolves
in
the
crude
oil
and
formation
water,
the
nano-agent
alters
the
oil
–
water
interfacial
tension
and
wettability,
and
the
reservoir
pressure,
effective
stress,
and
damage
state
are
reconfigured
accordingly;
and
(3)
well
opening
and
production,
in
which
crude
oil
and
the
mixed
fluid
are
produced.
Soaking
time
is
the
key
variable
linking
“
stimulation
performance
”
to
“
economic
viability
”
:
if
the
soak
is
too
short,
CO
2
has
not
yet

deep
into
the
matrix

the
nano-agent
has
not
had
time
to
act
fully,
so
the
stimulation
extent
is
limited;
if
the
soak
is
too
long,
operation
and
capital-occupancy
costs
continue
to
rise
and
production
is
deferred,
so
the
net
present
value
(NPV)
may
decline.
The
injection
composition,
injection
rate,
total
injection
volume,
and
injection
timing
also
affect
both
the
production-enhancement
response
and
the
operation
cost,
and
must
be
weighed
quantitatively
within
a
unified
modeling
framework.
Injection
timing
τ
inj
:

2
+

τ

τ

0

Problem
1
During
